Pedigree vs Moggy

Show-ring breeds fill our feeds. The cats on our sofas are mostly strays, rescues and gifts. A look at the cats we admire and the cats we actually live with, in Australia and Malaysia.

Pedigree
A cat of a recognised breed, such as a Ragdoll or Persian, bred to a written standard with known parents.
Moggy
A cat of mixed or unknown ancestry, the everyday "domestic shorthair". The word is Australian and British slang.

01Where cats live

Southeast Asia is cat country

About four in ten people in Indonesia, the Philippines and Thailand own a cat.

Malaysia sits mid-table at 34%, level with Australia.

City-state Singapore is the outlier: only one person in ten owns a cat.

Most cats are found, not bought

Only one in four Australian cats was bought from a breeder or pet shop. In New Zealand it is about one in eight.

In Putrajaya, six in ten pets were strays taken in from the neighbourhood, and only 15% were bought.

The pedigree breeds we admire are the exception, not the rule.

02Where breeds come from

Most pedigree breeds were made in America

30 of the 67 breeds were developed in the United States and 8 in the United Kingdom. Southeast Asia gave the world six: Siamese, Korat, Khao Manee, Burmese, European Burmese and the Singapura. None was developed in Malaysia.

03What breeds are like

Most breeds weigh about five kilos

Short-coated and long-coated breeds both have a typical weight of about 5 kg.

Rex and hairless breeds are the lightweights, at about 3.6 kg. The heaviest, the Maine Coon and the Chausie, reach about 9 kg.

Long coats come with a gentler reputation

Long-coated breeds are described as gentle, calm or quiet far more often than short-coated ones.

Every rex and hairless breed is listed as affectionate. Few breeds in any group are called independent.

04What we want

Australia likes Ragdolls, Malaysia likes British Shorthairs

In the year to October 2026, Ragdoll made up 36% of searches for the five breeds in Australia, but 23% in Malaysia.

British Shorthair is the reverse: 30% in Malaysia, 16% in Australia.

Search interest shows what people want to look at, not what they own.

In Malaysia, Ragdoll has overtaken Persian

Five years ago Persian ranked third of the five breeds in Malaysia and Ragdoll fourth.

Ragdoll passed Persian in the year to October 2024 and has kept climbing.

In Australia nothing moved: the five breeds kept the same order in all five years.

The favourites are the calm ones, except the Bengal

The average breed scores 3.9 for energy. The Ragdoll, Maine Coon, British Shorthair and Persian all score 3 or less.

The Persian needs the most grooming: 5, against an average of 1.9.

The Bengal is the odd one out, scoring 5 for energy, intelligence, company and noise.

Affection is left out: 60 of 67 breeds score the maximum 5, so it cannot tell breeds apart.

The cats we search for are American

Three of the five favourite breeds were developed in the United States. They make up three quarters of these searches in Australia and over half in Malaysia, where the British Shorthair takes 30%. The moggies on our sofas come from down the street.
